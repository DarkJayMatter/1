Complete Guide to RBI-Registered NBFCs in India

This directory is the most complete publicly searchable list of RBI-registered NBFCs you'll find online — 9,102 entities pulled directly from the Reserve Bank of India's official registry as on March 31, 2026. Every Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) operating legally in India must hold a Certificate of Registration (CoR) issued by RBI under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act, 1934. That means if a financial company offering you loans or deposits is not in this list, it is not authorised by RBI — full stop.

The NBFC sector has grown into a ₹65+ lakh crore asset base, serving borrowers and investors that formal banks often don't reach — small businesses in tier-3 towns, first-time rural borrowers, self-employed individuals without formal income proof, and micro-entrepreneurs. Bajaj Finance, Muthoot Finance, Shriram Finance, HDFC Holdings — all NBFCs. None of them are banks. Understanding the difference matters before you borrow, invest, or verify.

What is an NBFC and Why Does It Matter?

An NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company) is licensed by the Reserve Bank of India under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act, 1934 to provide financial services — personal loans, gold loans, vehicle finance, home loans (via HFCs), microfinance, and infrastructure debt. What separates NBFCs from banks is simple but significant: they cannot issue demand deposits (no savings accounts, no current accounts), they don't participate in the NEFT/RTGS payment network, and their deposits — where permitted — carry no DICGC insurance.

Why do they matter? Because roughly 25% of India's total credit flows through NBFCs, not banks. They've built their strength by going where banks don't: Muthoot Finance and Manappuram in gold loans to rural Kerala; Shriram Finance in used commercial vehicle loans in South India; Five Star Business Finance in micro-SME credit in Tamil Nadu. The formalisation of credit for India's underserved segments runs largely through NBFCs, not the formal banking sector.

Types of NBFCs in India — Complete Classification

The RBI classifies NBFCs into multiple categories based on their primary activity and regulatory framework:

  • NBFC-ICC (Investment and Credit Company): The largest category covering 8,600+ entities. Provides personal loans, vehicle loans, SME credit, gold loans, and general investment activities. Post-2019 RBI reclassification, the ICC category merged the earlier Asset Finance Company (AFC), Loan Company (LC), and Investment Company (IC) categories. All are included in this directory.
  • NBFC-MFI (Microfinance Institution): 95 registered entities. Lends to low-income households and economically weaker sections. RBI mandates that at least 85% of net assets must be qualifying microfinance loans. Key players include Spandana Sphoorty, Asirvad, Arohan, and CreditAccess Grameen.
  • HFC (Housing Finance Company): 84 registered entities. Primarily finances home purchase, construction, and renovation. At least 60% of net assets must be in housing finance. Examples: PNB Housing Finance, LIC Housing Finance, Can Fin Homes, Bajaj Housing Finance. HFCs were transferred from NHB (National Housing Bank) to RBI regulation in 2019.
  • NBFC-IFC (Infrastructure Finance Company): Provides long-term credit for infrastructure projects — roads, power, telecom, railways. Minimum 75% of assets must be in infrastructure loans. Includes entities like India Infrastructure Finance Company (IIFCL) and L&T Infrastructure Finance.
  • NBFC-IDF (Infrastructure Debt Fund): Raises resources through bond or unit issuance to refinance infrastructure project debt. Smaller category with 3 entities.
  • NBFC-CIC (Core Investment Company): Holds investments in group companies rather than conducting external lending. 59 entities. Subject to lighter regulation since they don\'t lend to the public.
  • NBFC-P2P (Peer to Peer Lending): 27 entities providing digital platforms connecting individual borrowers and lenders. RBI caps individual lender exposure at ₹50 lakh across all P2P platforms. Examples include 5Paisa P2P and 1Finance P2P.
  • NBFC-AA (Account Aggregator): 17 entities that act as data intermediaries, helping individuals share financial data across institutions in a consent-based framework. Core to India\'s Open Finance ecosystem.

Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) — The NBFC Layer System

In 2022, RBI introduced Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) — a transformative framework that classifies all NBFCs into four regulatory layers based on their size, complexity, and systemic importance. This replaced the earlier bifurcation between systemically important (SI) and non-systemically important NBFCs:

  • Base Layer (8,436 NBFCs): Smallest, lowest-risk entities. Lighter regulatory requirements. Many are single-product local lenders.
  • Middle Layer (624 NBFCs): Deposit-taking NBFCs, NBFC-MFIs, HFCs, IFCs, and larger non-deposit NBFCs. Subject to more stringent capital adequacy, leverage, and disclosure requirements.
  • Upper Layer (15 NBFCs): Specifically named by RBI as systemically important. Includes Bajaj Finance, LIC Housing Finance, Shriram Finance, Tata Motors Finance, Mahindra Finance, and others. Subject to near-bank-equivalent regulation including mandatory credit concentration limits and enhanced governance standards.
  • Top Layer: Currently empty. Reserved for NBFCs that pose extreme systemic risk — these entities may be required to convert into banks.

How to Verify an NBFC Before Dealing With It

Before taking a loan from or depositing money with any entity claiming to be an NBFC, always run these five checks:

  1. Search by name or CIN — use this directory to search the exact company name or its Corporate Identification Number (CIN). Fraudulent entities often use names similar to legitimate NBFCs.
  2. Match the CIN — the CIN shown in our results comes from the official RBI registry. If the company's CIN doesn't match, something is wrong.
  3. Cross-verify on RBI's website — go directly to the official RBI NBFC list and confirm the company appears there with the same name and CIN.
  4. Check the cancelled CoR list — visit the RBI's cancelled registration list. Companies whose Certificate of Registration (CoR) has been cancelled are no longer authorised to operate as NBFCs, even if they were previously legitimate.
  5. Verify deposit-taking status — only 23 out of 9,100+ NBFCs are legally authorised to accept public deposits. If a company asks you to deposit money but isn't shown as "Deposit-taking" in this directory, treat it as a potential fraud and report it to RBI.

NBFCs and Banking Tools You May Need

While NBFCs don\'t have IFSC codes of their own, many of their loan disbursals and repayments flow through bank accounts that do have IFSC codes. When repaying an NBFC loan via NEFT or RTGS, you\'ll need the IFSC code of the NBFC\'s designated bank branch. Use our IFSC Code Lookup to find it. For TDS deductions on NBFC-paid interest, the BSR Code of the deductor\'s bank branch is needed on the TDS challan. Frequently Asked Questions About NBFCs in India

An NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company) is a company registered under the Companies Act and licensed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) under Section 45-IA of the RBI Act, 1934 to carry out financial activities — lending, hire purchase, leasing, chit funds, and securities investment. NBFCs cannot accept demand deposits (savings/current accounts) and do not participate in the payment and settlement system (NEFT, RTGS). India has over 9,100 RBI-registered NBFCs as of March 2026.

Key differences: (1) Banks accept demand deposits — most NBFCs cannot. (2) Banks form part of NEFT/RTGS — NBFCs do not have IFSC codes. (3) Bank deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh — NBFC deposits have no such insurance. (4) Banks maintain CRR and SLR — NBFCs do not. (5) Banks are regulated under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 — NBFCs under the RBI Act, 1934.

Only NBFCs with explicit RBI authorisation — called NBFC-D (Deposit-taking) — can accept public deposits. As of March 2026, just 23 NBFCs in India have this authorisation. All others are NBFC-ND (Non-Deposit-taking). Critically, NBFC deposits are NOT insured under DICGC (which protects bank deposits up to ₹5 lakh). Exercise extreme caution before depositing with any NBFC. Always verify deposit-taking status in this directory.

An NBFC-MFI is a specialised NBFC that provides small, unsecured loans to low-income households. RBI mandates that at least 85% of net assets must be in qualifying microfinance loans. Individual household income caps apply: ₹3 lakh annual income for rural borrowers and ₹3.5 lakh for urban borrowers. India has 95 registered NBFC-MFIs.

An HFC is an NBFC that primarily finances home purchase, construction, and renovation. At least 60% of net assets must be in housing finance, with at least 50% in individual housing loans. India has 84 registered HFCs including PNB Housing Finance, Can Fin Homes, LIC Housing Finance, and Bajaj Housing Finance. HFCs were moved from NHB regulation to RBI regulation in 2019.

RBI's Scale-Based Regulation (2022) classifies NBFCs into 4 layers: Base Layer (8,436 NBFCs — small, low-risk, lighter regulation), Middle Layer (624 — deposit-taking NBFCs, MFIs, HFCs, larger lenders), Upper Layer (15 specifically named systemically important NBFCs — Bajaj Finance, LIC HFL, Shriram Finance etc.), and Top Layer (currently empty — reserved for extreme risk cases).

For loans from NBFCs: broadly safe if the NBFC is RBI-registered. For deposits with NBFCs: higher risk than bank deposits. NBFC deposits are NOT covered by DICGC insurance. If an NBFC fails, depositors may lose their money. Only deposit with NBFC-D authorised entities (just 23 as of 2026), and always check the NBFC's credit rating and latest RBI licence status.

An ARC purchases Non-Performing Assets (bad loans) from banks at a discount and works to recover them. ARCs are licensed by RBI under the SARFAESI Act, 2002. India has 27 registered ARCs — all included in this directory alongside the 9,075 NBFCs. Major ARCs include Edelweiss ARC, ARCIL, and Aditya Birla ARC.
