For Indian parents, the excitement around Grand Theft Auto VI (GTA 6) comes with a significant caveat. The game carries an 18+ classification on Indian PlayStation and Xbox storefronts, while the US-based Entertainment Software Rating Board has rated it Mature 17+. In the UK and Europe, it carries a PEGI 18 classification.

The differences reflect regional rating systems, but the message is consistent: GTA 6 is intended for a mature audience, not children. Its content warnings have renewed a global debate about violence in video games, children's exposure to adult themes and the responsibility of parents in an increasingly digital entertainment environment.

What makes GTA 6 different?

Set in the fictional state of Leonida, with Vice City at its centre, GTA 6 follows protagonists Jason and Lucia through a world of organised crime, robberies and police pursuits.

The open-world format gives players considerable freedom to interact with the virtual environment, including civilians and law enforcement officers. Its age-rating disclosures flag intense violence, blood, graphic injuries, nudity, sexual activity, drug and alcohol use, strong profanity and adult humour.

The PEGI classification also identifies frequent realistic violence, graphic injuries and prominent drug references. This makes GTA 6 fundamentally different from games designed for younger audiences.

Why are child-safety experts concerned?

The concerns extend beyond children playing the game themselves. Gameplay videos, livestreams and social media clips can expose younger audiences to scenes from GTA 6, even if they do not own it.

Guernsey Police's digital safety lead Laura Simpson has reportedly warned parents about the game's depictions of blood, violence, decapitations and unhealthy relationships. Guernsey is an island roighly 113 kilometre south of England.

Psychologist Nicholas Kardaras, speaking to ABC News, has also cautioned that parents may underestimate the intensity and realism of violence in the franchise. Parenting expert Catherine Hickem has highlighted the importance of considering a teenager's emotional maturity before allowing access to adult-oriented entertainment.

However, exposure to fictional violence does not automatically translate into aggressive behaviour. Research on violent video games has produced mixed findings, and there is no established evidence that playing such games inevitably makes children violent.

The concern is whether younger players can process the content appropriately and distinguish between virtual entertainment and real-world consequences.

Can violent games influence children's behaviour?

Younger teenagers may not have the emotional maturity to interpret graphic violence, sexualised behaviour and substance use in the same way as adults.

Parents should watch for changes such as aggressive language, imitating inappropriate behaviour, increased irritability or withdrawing from activities outside gaming. The more important question is whether exposure to mature content affects a child's behaviour and everyday interactions.

Is GTA 6 addictive?

GTA 6's expansive open world, missions and side activities can encourage extended gaming sessions. For some teenagers, this could make it difficult to disengage.

Parents should look for signs that gaming is interfering with sleep, academic performance, hobbies or family interactions. Irritability when asked to stop playing and an inability to follow agreed screen-time limits are also warning signs.

The focus should be on whether gaming is affecting a child's everyday routine rather than simply counting hours spent in front of a screen.

Does GTA 6 have online interactions?

At launch, GTA 6 is listed as a single-player experience, allowing players to follow its storyline independently rather than interact with other players through an online multiplayer mode.

However, children can still encounter GTA-related content through YouTube, livestreams and social media. The PlayStation India listing also identifies optional in-game purchases, making it important for parents to review account permissions and restrict unauthorised transactions.

What should parents do?

The game's age classification should be the starting point for any decision. With an 18+ rating on Indian storefronts, parents should take its content warnings seriously, particularly when considering access for younger teenagers.

They should review the official content descriptors and gameplay footage to understand what the game depicts. PlayStation and Xbox offer parental controls to restrict age-inappropriate games, manage screen time and limit purchases.

For older teenagers, conversations about violence, criminal behaviour, substance use and the difference between fiction and reality are equally important. Parents should also monitor changes in sleep, academic performance and behaviour.

Should parents allow GTA 6?

GTA 6's popularity means children may encounter its characters and gameplay even without owning a copy. But popularity should not determine whether a child gets access.

Its 18+ Indian classification and extensive content warnings indicate that it is intended for adults. Parents of younger teenagers should be cautious about allowing access, while decisions involving older teenagers should take their maturity and ability to understand the distinction between virtual entertainment and real-world behaviour into account.

Ultimately, parental involvement must go beyond setting screen-time limits. Understanding what children play, what they watch and how they respond to it is just as important.

]]> <![CDATA[How Dhruv Sayani built Ccigmaa Lifestyles into a five-brand personal care business]]> https://yourstory.com/2026/11/how-dhruv-sayani-built-ccigmaa-lifestyles-into-a-five-brand-personal-care-business https://yourstory.com/2026/11/how-dhruv-sayani-built-ccigmaa-lifestyles-into-a-five-brand-personal-care-business Thu, 19 Nov 2026 13:24:51 GMT

Hair and water composition can change significantly every 100 kilometres across India, affecting how haircare products perform. It was this observation, made while researching premature greying in his 20s, that led Dhruv Sayani to build Ccigmaa Lifestyles, a Mumbai-based personal care company.

Sayani found that keratin, the protein that makes up most of human hair, responds differently to these regional variations, something he believed conventional haircare formulations did not adequately address.

In 2018, he launched KT Professional, a salon-focused haircare brand developed specifically for stylists. Since then, Ccigmaa Lifestyles has expanded its portfolio with three more brands, KT Men, KT Kids and KT Derma, each focused on specific personal care needs.

The company, founded in 2018, aims to inspire “millions of minds” through Indian-made personal care products, according to Sayani.

Build credibility before distribution

Proving the regional-formulation premise required evidence a lab alone could not generate.

Rather than build retail distribution first and let salons discover the brand afterward, Ccigmaa Lifestyles went the other way around, giving hairstylists across Indian cities access to formulations, and letting their feedback, not a marketing brief, decide what shipped outside the production.

KT Professional was launched with Rs 30 lakh of the founder’s personal savings in 2018, exactly that route. Nearly a decade later, the company works with more than 27,000 salons across 86 cities, a distribution footprint built one stylist and salon relationship at a time rather than through a single national launch mode.

Turn specialization into a growth engine

Once KT Professional’s salon-first model was validated, the more conventional path was to push that single brand harder, deeper into more cities, more product lines, more advertising.

Sayani chose a different growth mechanism instead: apply the same underlying logic, that a mass-market, one-size-fits-all product underserves a large share of its actual buyers, to categories beyond haircare.

That decision produced KT Men and KT Kids as standalone brands rather than product extensions, a materially harder path since each requires its own credibility, its own distribution relationships, and its own reason to exist, rather than borrowing KT Professional's. It is also, by most available market evidence, the better-timed one.

Redseer projects India’s beauty and personal care market reaching $40 billion by 2030, positioning India as the fourth largest such market globally, with Gen Z and Alpha consumers becoming the largest beauty-spending cohort in that growth. A market moving toward younger, more specific buyers rewards brands built around a defined need far more than it rewards a single brand trying to serve everyone.

Position KT Men around performance, not vanity

Sayani says most Indian grooming brands have built their positioning around attraction or plain cosmetic vanity rather than performance, a framing he believed increasingly out of step with younger, fitness-oriented male consumers.

KT Men was built to compete on the second premise instead of the first, launching at an accessible Rs 399 entry point before extending into a higher Rs 699 to Rs 999 band through its G.O.A.T Edition range, a licensing partnership with the Argentina Football Association built around Lionel Messi, Julian Alvarez and Enzo Fernandez.

The brand has since disclosed a Rs 36 Crore marketing investment behind that range, against a Rs 200 crore revenue target over 18 to 24 months, sitting on top of KT Men’s original Rs 100 crore first-year ambition. Whether that specific target lands on schedule matters less to the decision itself than the positioning bet underneath it, that performance, not image, was the underused argument in Indian men's grooming.

Design for two customers at once

Children’s personal care carries a structural complication most categories do not: the person buying the product and the person using it are rarely evaluating it the same way.

A parent checks an ingredient list and asks whether a formulation is genuinely mild enough for a child's skin. A child has no interest in either question and decides on nothing more sophisticated than whether the experience of using it was enjoyable.

Rather than let KT Professional’s existing haircare range absorb a children's line as a smaller variant, Ccigmaa built KT Kids as its own brand, formulated specifically for children rather than adapted from adult products, and has run two entertainment-licensing partnerships in 15 months, first with SpongeBob SquarePants, then with Peppa Pig, timed separately rather than bundled, each aimed at a distinct age band.

The decision to treat that dual-audience problem as its own category, rather than a footnote inside an existing one, is the same underlying instinct that produced KT Men, applied to a harder version of the same question.

The company created five brands with one method to connect them: identify a specific need a generalist product is failing to serve, validate the idea against real, direct feedback before scaling it, and build a dedicated brand around it rather than stretch an existing one to cover it.

Ccigmaa's next test is whether that method holds as a repeatable business engine rather than a run of good individual calls, since a Rs 40 billion market moving toward specificity will reward the companies that can keep making this decision correctly, not just the ones that made it once.

For anyone interested in healthy ageing, the useful takeaway is not to copy his entire routine. It is to identify the simple habits that can fit into an ordinary lifestyle.

7 Affordable longevity habits inspired by Johnson's approach

1. Make quality sleep a priority

Johnson considers sleep one of the foundations of his health routine. He follows a consistent bedtime and aims to get around eight hours of sleep. His approach highlights an important habit that does not require expensive products or technology.

Try going to bed and waking up at roughly the same time every day. Keep your bedroom comfortable, reduce bright lights before bedtime and avoid scrolling on your phone when you should be winding down. Reading a book or listening to calming music can also help you establish a relaxing night-time routine.

2. Eat more whole foods

Johnson's diet places considerable emphasis on vegetables, berries, nuts, seeds and legumes. While his personalised meal plan is not necessary for everyone, the broader principle of choosing nutrient-rich foods is practical. Include seasonal fruits, vegetables, lentils, beans and whole grains in your meals.

Choose home-cooked food more often and reduce your dependence on heavily processed snacks and sugary drinks. These changes can be adapted to different budgets and food preferences without buying specialised longevity products.

3. Exercise regularly instead of chasing expensive treatments

Johnson combines strength training, cardio, flexibility and balance exercises in his fitness routine. However, building an active lifestyle does not require a personal trainer or an expensive gym membership.

Walking, cycling, dancing and bodyweight exercises can all help you stay active. Include activities that strengthen your muscles and improve cardiovascular fitness. Start with manageable sessions and gradually increase your activity according to your fitness level.

4. Get natural morning light

Morning light is another part of Johnson's approach to supporting the body's internal clock, also known as the circadian rhythm. Natural daylight helps regulate the sleep-wake cycle and provides a simple way to establish a healthier daily routine.

Spend some time outdoors after waking up, whether you take a short walk, sit on your balcony or enjoy your morning tea near an open space. You do not need specialised light therapy equipment if natural daylight is available. Avoid looking directly at the sun and protect your skin when UV levels are high.

5. Reduce screen time before bedtime

A healthy sleep routine is not just about the time you go to bed. It also involves preparing your mind and surroundings for rest. Johnson recommends switching off screens before bedtime and creating a calmer evening environment.

Try putting your phone away around an hour before sleeping. Turn off unnecessary notifications, reduce bright lighting and avoid work-related messages late at night. Replace late-night scrolling with reading, journaling or a relaxing activity that helps you disconnect from digital distractions.

6. Make time for relationships and social connections

Longevity is not only about food, exercise and sleep. Johnson also includes family, friendship and community among the habits that support a healthier life. Make an effort to stay connected with people who matter to you.

Spend time with family, meet friends regularly or participate in activities that help you build meaningful relationships. Even a simple conversation can make social connection a more intentional part of your routine.

Johnson uses extensive testing and health data to monitor his progress. Most people do not need advanced biological-age tests or expensive wearable devices to become more aware of their health. Final thoughts

Bryan Johnson's longevity routine may look out of reach because of its expensive treatments, specialised equipment and extensive testing. However, many of the habits behind his approach are accessible to people with ordinary budgets.

Prioritising sleep, eating balanced meals, staying active, getting natural daylight and maintaining meaningful relationships are practical starting points.

These habits do not promise to reverse ageing or guarantee a longer life, but they can support better overall health. The real lesson is to focus on sustainable changes rather than chasing every new longevity trend. You do not need to spend millions to start taking better care of your health.

The retirement fund body Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) on Thursday approved a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) for its retiring members to receive their accumulated provident fund savings in periodic instalments instead of withdrawing the entire corpus at once.

Under the SWP, retiring members of the EPFO will receive their accumulated provident fund savings in periodic installments instead of withdrawing the entire amount at once, a Labour Ministry release said.

The decision was taken at the 240th meeting of the Central Board of Trustees (CBT) of the Employees’ Provident Fund (EPF), chaired by Union Minister for Labour & Employment and Youth Affairs & Sports, Mansukh Mandaviya.

The Board approved a proposal to introduce a Systematic Withdrawal Plan, enabling retiring EPF members to receive their accumulated provident fund savings in periodic installments instead of withdrawing the entire amount at once.

This reform will enable retiring members to withdraw their accumulated provident fund savings at regular intervals, such as monthly, quarterly or half-yearly, based on their preference.

This facility would provide members with a structured source of periodic income while allowing the remaining corpus to continue generating returns, thereby helping augment their retirement income and supplement their pension benefits.

The facility would be entirely voluntary, with no compulsion on members to opt for withdrawals.

The option may also be extended to eligible family members/beneficiaries, ensuring greater flexibility and financial security for the family. This aims to ensure stable post-retirement income, reduce reinvestment risks, strengthen fund sustainability, and extend social security coverage to gig and platform workers.

SWP will enable members to convert their retirement savings into a stable and predictable stream of systematic withdrawals post-retirement, while preserving ownership of the underlying corpus.

Approximately 6.32 lakh EPF members aged above 55 years with provident fund accumulations exceeding Rs 5 lakh are expected to be the primary beneficiaries of the proposed SWP framework during the initial phase.

Bike taxi services could soon see a revival in Karnataka after the Supreme Court rejected the state government's appeal seeking to reinstate the ban on the service.

The decision removes a major legal hurdle for app-based platforms such as Rapido, Ola and Uber, potentially restoring a transport option that has gained relevance in Bengaluru's traffic-heavy urban landscape.

Bike taxis offer commuters a relatively quick and affordable alternative to autorickshaws and cabs, particularly for short-distance journeys. Their ability to navigate congested roads makes them useful for office-goers, students and those travelling to and from public transport hubs.

They could also help address last-mile connectivity gap.

Despite the expansion of the Namma Metro network in Bengaluru, several residential areas and workplaces remain some distance from stations. Bike taxis can bridge this gap, making Metro and BMTC services more accessible.

The legal dispute dates back to April 2025, when a single-judge bench of the Karnataka High Court ruled that aggregators could not operate bike taxi services without the state notifying the necessary guidelines. Following the order, services were suspended from June 16, 2025.

On January 23, 2026, a division bench of the Karnataka High Court overturned the earlier ruling. As reported by Bar and Bench, the court held that motorcycles could be registered as transport vehicles and operated as contract carriages under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. It also directed transport authorities to consider applications for vehicle registration and contract carriage permits.

The Karnataka government subsequently challenged the order before the Supreme Court, citing concerns over passenger safety, insurance coverage and the lack of a clear regulatory framework.

Dismissing the state's appeal, the Supreme Court observed that bike taxis should be regulated rather than prohibited, recognising their affordability and convenience for commuters.

Welcoming the development, Uber said the decision would bring greater regulatory clarity to the sector.

“We welcome the Hon’ble Supreme Court’s decision to uphold the Karnataka High Court’s judgment on bike taxis. The decision brings much-needed regulatory clarity and recognises bike taxis as an important mobility option, offering commuters an affordable and convenient way to navigate congested cities while creating flexible earning opportunities for drivers,” an Uber spokesperson said.

However, the ruling does not give aggregators an unconditional licence to operate. They will still need to comply with applicable regulatory requirements, including vehicle registration and contract carriage permits.

For Bengaluru, a regulated bike taxi ecosystem could strengthen last-mile connectivity and create livelihood opportunities. However, concerns around road safety, passenger insurance, rider verification and aggregator accountability will need to be addressed.

The next step rests with Karnataka's transport department. The pace at which authorities process applications and implement the regulatory requirements will determine when and how bike taxis return to Bengaluru's roads.

Phishing is the most frequently reported cyber incident among businesses surveyed in Asia-Pacific, but the sharper warning for India is that attacks are exploiting weaknesses in employee awareness as well as technology. 

In a Kaspersky survey, 28% of Indian businesses said they had experienced phishing between March 2025 and March 2026. India shares the highest reported proportion with Indonesia, above the APAC average of 24%.

The survey covered 1,800 interviews across 18 countries, including 100 Indian respondents. 

In India, 21% reported attacks involving weak or stolen credentials, compared with 15% across APAC. Mass malware attacks were reported by 21%, against 18% regionally. Software vulnerability exploits were at 20%, slightly below APAC's 21%. 

Among APAC respondents whose organisations experienced cyber incidents, 33% said attackers sought sensitive business information and 32% identified customers’ personal data as a target during their most harmful incident.

One of the most striking findings concerns employee awareness. About 70% of Indian respondents said their organisations had experienced at least one cyberattack exploiting employees’ low IT security awareness. That matched Malaysia and was above the APAC average of 63%, though below Vietnam’s 75%.

This sits uneasily beside another survey result. Some 53% of Indian respondents described their organisation’s cybersecurity culture as high, meaning employees were well-educated in IT security and followed the rules. The remaining 47% rated it average, indicating that staff knew basic practices but did not always act on them. None selected the lowest category.

“A phishing email can look like any other message on a busy workday, from a supplier's invoice to a request from a colleague. Employees may know what phishing is, but recognising it in everyday communications can be a challenge,” said Adrian Hia, managing director for Asia Pacific at Kaspersky.

Hia argues that businesses should move beyond basic instruction and give staff repeated opportunities to practise identifying suspicious requests through simulated attacks.

The threat is also becoming more sophisticated. In an advisory issued on August 7, 2026, India’s Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) warned of targeted attacks against Microsoft 365 environments using password-spraying, credential-stuffing, device-code phishing and stolen session tokens. 

Device-code phishing abuses a legitimate sign-in process to trick a user into authorising an attacker's access. The advisory also describes business email compromise, in which criminals take over or impersonate business email accounts to divert payments or obtain sensitive information.

India’s regulatory framework is evolving alongside these risks. CERT-In requires specified cyber incidents to be reported within six hours of being noticed and calls for ICT system logs to be retained securely for 180 days within India.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, notified in November 2025, provide for phased compliance and require organisations responsible for personal data to inform affected individuals without delay after a personal data breach. The Reserve Bank of India’s 2026 directions for commercial banks and non-bank payment operators also formalise controls around cyber resilience and digital payment security.
