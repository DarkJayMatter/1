What they build

Moody's Corp.

Moody's has two sides. Moody's Ratings gives credit ratings: opinions on how likely a borrower is to repay a bond or loan, on a letter scale from Aaa to C. An analyst team gathers the borrower's information and applies a published methodology, with models and scorecards. A rating committee then votes on the rating, which is published and then monitored. The other side, under the Moody's name, sells data and software for judging risk. It includes Orbis, a database of private and listed companies and their owners.

The problem it solves

Investors who buy bonds need an independent opinion on how likely a borrower is to repay. Banks, investors and companies also need information on private companies in many countries, set out in the same form and showing who in the end owns each one.

How it works

Ratings

At Moody's Ratings, a new rating starts when the borrower signs an agreement. An analyst team is assigned, and the borrower shares information and meets the analysts. The team applies the methodology for that kind of borrower.

Methodologies include models and scorecards as reference tools, but each committee applies its own judgement. The committee reviews, votes on and assigns the rating. The borrower reviews the draft press notice, and the rating is then published and monitored.

Scales and methods

Long-term ratings run from Aaa, the highest quality and lowest credit risk, down to C. Short-term ratings run from P-1 to P-3, with NP for Not Prime.

New or changed methodologies are published for comment before they become final. Published notes explain the reasoning behind each credit opinion.

Company data and AI

Orbis, from a company that Moody's bought in 2017, collects company data from many places and standardises financial statements. It applies standard names and industry codes, and links companies to their owners. These links include corporate trees and beneficial ownership, meaning the real people who ultimately own a company.

Customers reach Orbis through a web app, APIs and connectors, or by bulk feed and cloud delivery. Moody's builds AI agents into its data products.

Industry trend · From the 2010s

Software for listed shares and bonds added private investments

More money went into private equity, private credit and real estate. Software and data companies built for listed shares and bonds added accounting, data and valuation for these private holdings. They often did this by buying specialist firms.
